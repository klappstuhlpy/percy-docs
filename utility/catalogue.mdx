## The releases overview

`/releases` is the front door. It shows two lists — comics and film & TV — over a window you pick with the **Days** control: 7, 14 or 30.

The two halves do not cover the same span, and the page says so on each section:

- **Comics look backwards.** A comic appears from the day Percy first saw it, which is often months before it reaches a shop — publishers solicit issues early. A forward-looking comic list would be full of books nobody can buy yet.
- **Film & TV looks both ways.** Titles are placed on their release date, so the list covers the days just past and the days just ahead. The forward half is worth trusting because Percy re-checks the next month of dates against TMDB every night.

Signed in, a **Following** section leads the page with the entries your subscriptions match. It is a subset of what the page is already showing, never a separate list, and it is absent rather than empty when nothing matches.

## The news stream

`/news` is every story Percy has collected from the feeds it polls, newest first. Filter it by medium, by a single subject, by outlet, or by searching headlines. Paging is plain links, and the filters are a plain form, so the page works with JavaScript off.

Every story is shown the same way: **the headline, a short excerpt, the name of the outlet, and a link to the original**. Percy never reproduces an article — it points at one.

Signed in, the stories matching what you follow lead the same single list rather than appearing in a second box.

<Note>
  Percy tags a story with a subject only on an exact match against its catalogue, or a curated alias of one. It will miss stories rather than guess: a wrong tag would send a story to people who did not ask for it, which is worse than a story nobody sees.
</Note>

## The headline strip

The releases, comics and watch-order pages each carry a strip of recent headlines under the header, with **All news →** at the end. Signed in, it leads with stories about what you follow.

It is a real list of links, not decoration: it pauses when you hover it or move keyboard focus into it, and it stops moving entirely if your system asks for reduced motion. When Percy cannot be reached, the strip is simply not there — you never get an empty bar.

A single comic issue's page also carries a **related news** section: the most recent stories written about that series. It is absent when there are none.

## A page for every film and show

Every entry in a watch order is also a page of its own — reachable from the order, from a person's filmography, or from a release card. It carries the synopsis, where the title sits in its order (with **← Previous** and **Next →** to walk the order without going back to the list), where to stream it in your region, the full cast and crew, related news, and a trailer.

The trailer is a **link first**. Percy shows a play control that goes to the trailer on YouTube or Vimeo; clicking it swaps in an inline player on the page instead. Nothing is embedded until you ask for it — an embed pulls a few hundred kilobytes and sets cookies before you have decided you want it — and nothing ever autoplays: you press play once to get the player and again to start it. A middle-click or Cmd/Ctrl-click opens the trailer at the source, like any other link.

<Note>
  Percy stores one trailer per title, picked from everything TMDB publishes for it: a full trailer beats a teaser, an official upload beats a repost, and the original-language version beats a dub. Plenty of titles have none — an unreleased film often has nothing yet — and that section is simply absent rather than empty.
</Note>

## Marking what you have read and watched

Signed in, every release card, comic issue, watch-order row and detail page carries a **Read** or **Watched** control. Clicking it marks the item; clicking it again unmarks it. Marks are personal, not per-server: they follow you across every page and every device you sign in on, and nobody else sees them. If two devices disagree about an item, the more recent change wins.

A watch order also shows a **progress bar** — *N of M watched*, counted over exactly the entries listed under it, so filtering the order to one path re-counts against that path.

On any list you can move with the keyboard: <kbd>j</kbd> and <kbd>k</kbd> step down and up through the entries, and <kbd>w</kbd> marks the one you are on. The hint only appears once the shortcuts are actually available.

<Note>
  The controls are ordinary buttons in ordinary forms. With JavaScript off they still work — the click posts the mark and returns you to where you were. With JavaScript on, the page updates immediately and the write is batched a moment later, so marking a run of issues is one request rather than ten. If a write fails, the control says so rather than quietly keeping the mark on your device only.
</Note>

Marking is signed-in only; signed out, the control is a **Sign in to track** link instead. Nothing else on the page changes.

## Following from the web

Release cards and news stories are labelled with the subjects they were matched on — a publisher, a series, a universe — and the ones with a catalogue page of their own link to it. The [Subscriptions page](https://percy.klappstuhl.me/account/subscriptions) is where you turn any of them into a follow. See [Subscriptions](/utility/subscriptions) for what following something gets you.

<Tip>
  Film and TV data comes from TMDB, comic data from League of Comic Geeks, and every news story from the outlet named on it. Each page credits its source and links out — follow the link when you want the full article.
</Tip>
